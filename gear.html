Tomes

Five that changed how I run

Most game master advice is either a restatement of the rulebook or a lecture on how you're doing it wrong. These five aren't. Each one changed something concrete about how I prep or how I sit at the table — not how I feel about the hobby. I own all of them.

ItemDescription/Purpose
So You Want To Be A Game MasterJustin AlexanderThe one I'd hand somebody who has never run a session and is scared to. Structured, practical, and it never once tells you that you're the god of your world.
Play DirtyJohn Wick · 15th Anniversary EditionArgumentative, occasionally wrong, and worth more than three polite books put together. You will disagree with parts of it. That's the point — it makes you say out loud why you run the way you do.
The Monsters Know What They're DoingKeith AmmannWritten with 5e in mind, yes, but still valuable for any fantasy game that uses the monsters Keith highlights. Not every monster is the proverbial nail to the players’ hammer.
Play UnsafeGraham WalmsleyThe shortest book here and the best value per page. Improv principles aimed at people who prep too much — which is most of us, me included.
Return of the Lazy Dungeon MasterMichael E. SheaCuts prep down to what actually gets used at the table. If you've ever built a dungeon nobody entered, this book is about you and it is not unkind about it.

GM Gear

At the table

The stuff on my side of the screen. Most of it is office supplies, and none of it is fancy. It's what I actually reach for mid-session.

ItemDescription/Purpose
Legal padsEasy to jot down details at the table in the moment.
CardstockTable tents for home games or cons. Preprint them and make the PC name, class, etc. big enough to read!
Sand timersSpeed up combat, limit player delays. Put these on the table.
Dry-erase tokens5 goblins? Put G1, G2, G3… on each and put them on the mat! Easy.
Index cardsUsed for everything from adventure prep to initiative counters on top of the GM screen.
Index card caseHelps sort your cards: monsters, items, room descriptions, etc.
Sticky notesPass notes at the table, stick one inside the GM screen for when a spell ends, and more.
Pen loopGreat for journals to hold your pens and pencils.
Gel pensSmooth writing. I prefer the .7mm tip.
Mechanical pencilThe pencil of pen(cil), paper and dice!
JournalThere are cheaper knock-offs, but these are the best, IMO. I prefer dots over lined or blank.
Stencil bookmarkSpice up your journal. Great for players too: mapping, adding emphasis, drawing straight lines, etc.
DiceTHUD! Just don’t use them on your friend’s nice wooden table without a dice tray.
Dice traysDon’t be ‘that guy’ whose dice fly off the table, under the table.
BattlematThe mat to have if you like minis, positioning, tactics.

Bardic Kit

What Saturday morning runs on

Nothing here is aspirational. This is the kit that actually produces 255 live streams and two podcasts, in a house with cats and dogs in it.

ItemDescription/Purpose
Audio
Electro-Voice RE320Large-diaphragm dynamic · Variable-DA dynamic mic hears what's in front of it and mostly ignores the rest of the room. When you record at home with rescue animals in the house, that's the whole ballgame.
Cloudlifter CL-1Inline mic activator · +25dBThe RE320 is a quiet microphone. This sits between it and the interface and gives it the gain it needs without turning the preamp up until you can hear the furnace. If you own a low-output dynamic and your recordings sound thin and hissy, this is the missing piece, and I wish I'd bought one sooner.
